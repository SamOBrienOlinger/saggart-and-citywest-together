About Saggart & Citywest Together

Saggart & Citywest Together is a volunteer-led group of local residents working to make our communities welcoming places to live.

What we doHow we workHow the area has changedHow we challenge racismSources we use

What we do

We help people join in, support fair access to local services, and work to make Saggart and Citywest safe and welcoming. We respect people’s dignity, identities and right to participate. The group has no political affiliation.

How we work

Mission statement

Saggart & Citywest Together is a group of locals striving to see our communities flourish and thrive through collective action. By encouraging engagement from all ages and backgrounds, we can lobby for better services and access in the local area while making Saggart & Citywest safe, fun, and vibrant places to live for all residents.

We are volunteer-led and, as a group, have no political affiliation.

Resident-led

People living in Saggart and Citywest can suggest ideas and start activities that help the community.

Inclusive and respectful

Everyone should be treated with dignity and respect. We challenge racism and discrimination and consider the needs of the whole community.

Open and accountable

The committee works towards agreement, holds an annual general meeting (AGM) and keeps financial records. It has rules to prevent personal interests from affecting group decisions.

Safe and welcoming

S&CT is committed to protecting children, young people and adults at risk, taking concerns seriously and working with relevant agencies where needed.

Community in action

How the area has changed

From the early community around Teach Sacra, through farming, the Swift Brook Paper Mill and the Blessington steam tram, Saggart has always been connected to wider change. Citywest, the N7 and the Luas brought another period of rapid growth. Today, people with deep local roots and people who have made their home here more recently (whether from elsewhere in Ireland or from other countries) share the area.

Understanding this history helps us discuss change in a grounded, respectful way and work together for a safe, welcoming and vibrant community.

How we challenge racism

Racism can happen between people and through the rules and everyday practices of organisations. It can happen even when people do not intend it. We use clear, respectful language and avoid unfair assumptions about groups of people. We listen to people who experience racism and support their equal say in community life.

If you experience or witness racism in Ireland, you can confidentially record it through iReport.ie (opens in a new tab) and find practical guidance through INAR’s Responding to Racism Guide (opens in a new tab). Call 999 or 112 where there is an immediate danger.

How we check our content

Information about the group comes from its agreed rules and community plans. We use guidance from the Irish Network Against Racism (INAR) to explain racism, migration and inclusion clearly and respectfully. Local history and civic content uses public information from South Dublin County Council, the Placenames Database of Ireland and official heritage resources. Population figures come from the Central Statistics Office’s Census 2022 reports. We say which area each figure covers, because town, county and neighbourhood boundaries differ. Those organisations have not formally reviewed this website, and we do not speak for them.
