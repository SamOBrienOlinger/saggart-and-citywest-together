# Facts: editorial and source notes

Reviewed 19 September 2026. Public page: `facts.html`. The page is static HTML; all educational content and native disclosure controls work without JavaScript.

## Selection

The ten migration/anti-racism resources combine direct myth-busting, Irish evidence, terminology, comparative research and practical anti-rumour methods. They are a recommendation for this community, not a claim to an objective global ranking. Selection considered publisher expertise, evidence/methodology, Irish relevance, accessibility and currency. Research is distinguished from rights guidance and educational methods. The resource register records the ten choices and their limits; the public page is the content source of truth.

The developmenteducation.ie PDF credits confirm publication in 2021. Its old statistics and legal explanations are explicitly not presented as current. The Irish Refugee Council hub separates applications before and from 12 June 2026; the page directs readers to that distinction instead of reproducing procedural advice. ESRI's *Monitoring Report on Integration 2024* was published in March 2025, not 2024. OECD's 2025 Outlook was published in November 2025. Oxford's UK labour-market briefing was updated in February 2026. FRA's 2023 report uses 2021–2022 fieldwork for a defined population and includes Ireland; it is not an all-migrants or local survey.

The two Council of Europe migration resources are different: the 2018 handbook supports community strategy; the 2021 dialogue toolkit supports individual conversations. Both are methods, not current statistical sources. The information-disorder framework is from 2017; UNESCO's handbook is from 2018 even though the webpage was updated in 2024. Definitions and practical checks are original summaries with direct links, not copied passages. The three literacy resources serve everyday readers, deeper verification practice and Irish youth education respectively.

UNHCR Ireland Help was considered but explicitly warned that its content was under review and might be outdated. It was not included as current guidance. Several otherwise reputable sources (including IOM and an older Doras campaign) could not be reliably retrieved; no inaccessible resource was selected solely because of its publisher. Nasc's 2024 immigration guide was considered but predates the June 2026 changes. These exclusions concern the suitability of particular pages for this selection, not the credibility of the organisations.

## Local CSO coverage

The directory includes Census 2022 SAPS (all 15 themes), the interactive census map, urban profiles, the Saggart population discussion, age table F4014, summary results, all eight thematic profiles, volunteering, boundary methodology and historical 2016/2011 SAPS plus the census archive. Profile 6 is explicitly wider context, not local homelessness data. The collection also links geographical income (2022 and 2016 collection), new dwelling completions, property prices, electricity-based residential vacancy, Electoral Division/health profiles and POWSCCAR research methodology/access. Series landing pages expose revisions and earlier releases without duplicating every monthly or quarterly edition.

Saggart Built Up Area, Saggart Electoral Division, the community understood as Citywest, South Dublin county, Dublin region, Eircode routing keys and service catchments are not interchangeable. No unsupported Citywest total, catchment or Small Area identifier was invented. Combining Citywest Small Areas requires an explicitly defined, non-overlapping boundary. Census 2022 is a 3 April 2022 snapshot; it does not measure current IPAS occupancy or establish immigration status. CSO says 2022 Built Up Areas are not directly comparable with 2016 settlement boundaries.

Income 2022 was published 23 April 2025 and is a Frontier Series output with linkage/coverage limits; its report provides town and Electoral Division estimates. New dwelling completions provides LEA and routing-key tables. Residential vacancy 2024 was published 24 March 2026; the page uses it only as South Dublin context. The POWSCCAR records require researcher approval, so the public page does not promise unrestricted downloads. Historical 2011 SAPS links to downloadable tables but its legacy map references Flash; readers are warned about the old mapping software.

The directory covers the relevant verified publication families and their archives. It does not claim that every CSO release contains separate Saggart and Citywest figures. Dataset geography, measures and dates must be checked before drawing a local conclusion.

## Maintenance

Review dates refer to this editorial check, not an automatic monitoring service. Recheck living guidance when rules change, annual research when a new edition appears, and CSO release collections as updates arrive. Use the visible correction route for reader feedback. Do not silently replace historical figures with current ones or infer individual characteristics from area statistics.

Browser coverage adds Facts to every primary-page/reflow sweep, to 200% text checks and actual desktop Safari. The new scenario checks navigation, the 10/3/3 resource groups, local-data jump navigation, publication disclosure and screenshots. No-JavaScript coverage also navigates to Facts and opens historical data. Live external resource content was researched separately; browser tests do not pretend to validate third-party services.
