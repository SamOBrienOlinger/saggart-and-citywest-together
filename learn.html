Place and identity

Meet two connected communities at the meeting point of historic village, growing neighbourhood and Dublin Mountains landscape.

Historic village

Saggart village

Saggart is an historic village in the foothills of the Dublin Mountains, south of the N7. Its village form, older buildings and long history of milling remain important to local identity.

Source: South Dublin County Council: Saggart Village (opens in a new tab)

Growing neighbourhood

Citywest and Fortunestown

Citywest and Fortunestown form a newer residential and employment district east of Saggart. Planning for the area connects housing growth with public transport, services and public spaces.

Source: South Dublin County Council: Citywest, Saggart, Rathcoole, Newcastle (opens in a new tab)

Irish place name

Teach Sagard

The Placenames Database of Ireland records Teach Sagard as the Irish form of Saggart. Using official place names helps preserve the language and history carried by the landscape.

Source: Placenames Database of Ireland: Teach Sagard / Saggart (opens in a new tab)

People and population

Census figures tell us about people living in an area at a particular time. Check the area covered before comparing numbers.

Saggart Built Up Area

A young local population

Census 2022 counted 4,573 people in the Saggart Built Up Area. Its average age was 30.4 years, making Saggart the youngest Irish town in the 1,500 to 10,000 population category. The area counted by the CSO does not exactly match the full Saggart and Citywest community.

Source: Central Statistics Office: Census 2022 Profile 1 — Population Distribution (opens in a new tab)

Saggart Built Up Area

Age groups

Of the 4,573 people counted, 1,529 were aged under 15 (33.4%), 377 were 15–24 (8.2%), 1,594 were 25–44 (34.9%), 795 were 45–64 (17.4%) and 278 were 65 or older (6.1%). Percentages are calculated from the CSO’s five-year age groups and rounded to one decimal place.

Source: Central Statistics Office: Census 2022 Table F4014 — Population by Age Group and Town (opens in a new tab)

South Dublin context

Families and households

Across South Dublin, Census 2022 recorded 76,623 families, an average of 1.41 children per family and an average household size of 2.97 people. These county-level figures provide context; they are not estimates for Saggart or Citywest alone.

Source: Central Statistics Office: Census 2022 Profile 3 — Households, Families and Childcare: Dublin (opens in a new tab)

Small Area data

Explore your neighbourhood

Citywest is not separately named in the CSO town table used for this snapshot. The Census interactive map lets you explore published data at neighbourhood and Small Area level without counting the same area twice.

Source: Central Statistics Office: Census 2022 Interactive Map (opens in a new tab)

The stories behind the places

Discover the traditions, landmarks and working lives that helped shape the area.

Historical tradition

An early Christian story

Saggart is said to take its name from Saint Sacer, also recorded as Mosacer or Mosacra, who is traditionally associated with a seventh-century monastery in the area.

Source: South Dublin County Council: South Dublin County Heritage Plan (opens in a new tab)

Saggart village

Crosses and burial ground

Stone crosses attributed to the early Christian period can be seen in the graveyard in Saggart village, making it an important surviving link with the area’s long history.

Source: South Dublin County Council: South Dublin County Heritage Plan (opens in a new tab)

Local industry

Milling and village life

Saggart has a long association with milling. That working history forms part of the village story alongside its main street, church, houses and routes through southwest County Dublin.

See the mill photograph

Source: South Dublin County Council: Saggart Village (opens in a new tab)

Growth and connections

Roads, public transport, jobs and new homes connect the old village with the growing neighbourhood around it.

Public transport

The Luas Red Line

The neighbourhood is served by Saggart, Fortunestown and Citywest Campus Luas stops, providing links through Tallaght to Dublin city centre and the Docklands.

Source: South Dublin County Council: Citywest, Saggart, Rathcoole, Newcastle (opens in a new tab)

Planning

Homes, jobs and services

Citywest Business Campus and nearby employment areas play an important economic role. A growing area also needs community centres, shops, schools and public spaces that everyone can use.

Source: South Dublin County Council: Citywest, Saggart, Rathcoole, Newcastle (opens in a new tab)

Saggart

A connected village

County planning describes Saggart as closely connected to growing Citywest and Fortunestown while supporting new development that improves the village centre and supports walking and cycling.

Source: South Dublin County Council: Saggart — Self-Sustaining Town (opens in a new tab)

Nature and landscape

The hills, valleys, hedgerows and parks provide homes for wildlife and places for people to enjoy.

Landscape

Dublin Mountains foothills

Saggart sits at the foothills of the Dublin Mountains. Nearby routes provide outdoor access and broad views across the city and county.

Source: South Dublin County Council: Saggart Village (opens in a new tab)

Walking

Saggart Hill loop

The waymarked Saggart Hill loop is approximately 3.8 kilometres and graded easy by South Dublin County Council’s walking guidance.

Source: South Dublin County Council: Walking and Hiking (opens in a new tab)

Plants and wildlife

Slade Valley and green links

Council planning identifies Slade of Saggart and Crooksling Glen for their natural heritage value and explains how hedgerows connect wildlife habitats and form part of local history.

Source: South Dublin County Council: Citywest, Saggart, Rathcoole, Newcastle (opens in a new tab)

Community and belonging

People feel more at home when they share local spaces, can use services and have a say in the area’s future.

Local services

Community spaces

Parks, playgrounds and community centres help turn new development into sustainable neighbourhoods. Council planning identifies facilities such as Carrigmore Green, Citywest playground and Saggart Community Centre.

Source: South Dublin County Council: Citywest, Saggart, Rathcoole, Newcastle (opens in a new tab)

Community development

Making it easier for everyone to join in

Everyone should be able to use local services and have a say in decisions, whatever their age or background, including people from minority ethnic communities.

Source: South Dublin County Council: Community Development (opens in a new tab)

Anti-racism

Responding to racism

Racism can happen between people, in organisations and through wider rules and practices. INAR provides guidance for people who experience or witness racism, including how to seek support, report incidents and take action as a community.

Source: Irish Network Against Racism: Responding to Racism Guide (opens in a new tab)
