01 / Understand migration

Migration, racism and the evidence

Ten recommended resources for challenging migration myths and racial stereotypes. Start with a clear explanation, then follow the original evidence.

Why these ten?

We prioritised expertise, traceable evidence, Irish relevance, accessible explanations and a clear publication date or update path. This is a curated selection for our community, not a universal ranking.

The collection brings together direct myth-busting, original research, definitions and practical anti-racism tools. Each serves a different purpose. Older learning materials are labelled, and evidence from another country is kept in context.

Apply the same checks to every source, including sources you agree with. A claim needs evidence; people’s dignity does not depend on their economic contribution.

  1. developmenteducation.ie

    2021 · Ireland & international · PDF

    10 Myths About Migration & Refugees ↗

    Start with common claims

    An accessible, referenced teaching booklet by Tony Daly and Ciara Regan. Use its questions to examine migration claims and discuss the people behind the statistics.

    Background reading: its statistics and asylum rules are historical. Use current sources below to check today’s figures and procedures.

  2. Irish Refugee Council

    Living resource · Ireland · Information hub

    Information Hub ↗

    Check the asylum process

    Refugee-rights guidance that helps separate the international protection process from rumours. The hub provides different information for applications made before, or on or after, 12 June 2026.

    Choose the route that matches the application date. Follow the publisher’s updates; general information does not resolve an individual legal case.

  3. Economic and Social Research Institute

    Published March 2025 · Ireland · Research

    Monitoring Report on Integration 2024 ↗

    Check claims about integration

    National survey and administrative evidence on employment, education, social inclusion and citizenship. A strong starting point for checking broad claims about migrants’ lives in Ireland.

    Read each indicator’s reference year. “Foreign-born”, “non-Irish citizen” and “person seeking asylum” describe different groups.

  4. Irish Network Against Racism

    Resource collection · Ireland · Anti-racism learning

    Learn About Racism in Ireland ↗

    Understand how racism works

    Definitions, learning materials and research from Ireland’s anti-racism network. Helps identify stereotyping and understand how discrimination can operate through institutions as well as individual behaviour.

    Anti-racist education and a route to evidence; this is not a population count or a migration statistics release.

  5. European Migration Network / European Commission

    Living reference · European Union · Glossary

    EMN Asylum and Migration Glossary ↗

    Use precise words

    A multilingual reference for migration and asylum terminology. Check what a term means before comparing figures or repeating a claim that mixes different migration categories.

    Definitions have a stated EU or international context. They do not determine a particular person’s immigration status.

  6. Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development

    November 2025 · OECD countries · Research

    International Migration Outlook 2025 ↗

    Put migration figures in context

    Comparative evidence on migration, work and integration, with country notes and a statistical annex. Useful for testing sweeping international comparisons and claims about migrant workers.

    Use the country, migration category and data year attached to each table. An OECD average is not an Irish or local figure.

  7. University of Oxford · Migration Observatory

    Updated February 2026 · United Kingdom · Evidence briefing

    The Labour Market Effects of Immigration ↗

    Look closely at jobs and wages

    Explains research on employment and wages, including why effects differ between groups and why measuring cause and effect is difficult. Links to the studies behind the analysis.

    UK evidence: useful for understanding the question, but it cannot establish the size of an effect in Saggart, Citywest or Ireland.

  8. EU Agency for Fundamental Rights

    2023 report · 2021–2022 fieldwork · 13 EU countries

    Being Black in the EU ↗

    Listen to evidence about discrimination

    Survey evidence on racism experienced by people of African descent, including respondents in Ireland. The report provides its questionnaire and technical information for readers to examine.

    The surveyed population is specific. These findings do not describe every migrant, every minority ethnic group or this local community.

  9. Council of Europe · Intercultural Cities

    2018 · International practice · PDF

    Antirumours Handbook ↗

    Build a community response

    A structured method for identifying rumours, gathering evidence and involving residents in challenging prejudice. Particularly useful for community groups planning sustained anti-racism work.

    A practical methodology with historical examples, rather than a source of current migration totals.

  10. Council of Europe · Intercultural Cities

    2021 · International practice · PDF

    Claiming the Power of Dialogue ↗

    Have a constructive conversation

    A shorter toolkit for responding to stereotypes and misinformation in conversation. Covers listening, building a connection, correcting a claim and reflecting on what helped.

    Use alongside sound evidence and respect for people’s dignity. A conversation toolkit cannot verify a factual claim on its own.

02 / Know the area

CSO data for Saggart and Citywest

Start with the Central Statistics Office’s local census tables, maps and publications. Use the full Census 2022 programme below to explore all available topics.

Choose the right boundary before quoting a number

  • Saggart: the Built Up Area and the Electoral Division cover different boundaries.
  • Citywest: use the map to identify the Small Areas covering the neighbourhood you mean. Do not relabel Saggart’s town total as a Citywest total.
  • South Dublin: county figures provide context. They are not figures for either neighbourhood; “Dublin” may refer to a wider area again.

Census 2022 describes 3 April 2022. It is not a current IPAS resident count. Birthplace, citizenship, ethnicity and religion do not establish someone’s asylum or immigration status.

Includes population, migration and ethnicity, religion, Irish, families, households, housing, volunteering, work, social class, education, commuting, health and care, occupations, industries, cars and internet access.

Income, housing and other local statistical sources

These sources use different boundaries and reference periods. Select the geography that covers your location; a routing key or Local Electoral Area is wider than an individual neighbourhood.

  • Geographical Profiles of Income in Ireland 2022 — Household Income

    Published April 2025. Town and Electoral Division estimates, including the geography needed to investigate Saggart and the divisions covering Citywest. This is a Frontier Series release: linked administrative income has coverage limitations. Read the background notes before comparing areas.

  • Geographical Profiles of Income — release collection

    Access the 2016 and 2022 publications and their tables. Do not assume identical boundaries or methods across years.

  • New Dwelling Completions

    Quarterly releases, with annual and quarterly tables by Local Electoral Area and Eircode routing key. Useful housing-supply context; these are dwellings completed, not the number of people moving into an area.

  • Residential Property Price Index and property prices by Eircode

    The publication collection links to the interactive Eircode tool. Routing-key transaction prices describe a wider area and should not be presented as the value of every home in Saggart or Citywest.

  • Residential Vacancy Based on Metered Electricity Consumption 2024

    Published March 2026. Includes South Dublin local-authority context. Its electricity-based vacancy measure differs from census-night vacancy; it is not a neighbourhood count of homes available to rent.

  • Census 2022 Electoral Division and health-area profiles

    Select the relevant Electoral Division or health-area boundary. A service catchment is not necessarily a town boundary.

  • Place of Work, School, College or Childcare census records

    Methodology and research access for detailed commuting analysis. Access to the anonymised records requires CSO approval; use the public Profile 7 and SAPS tables above for everyday reference.

CSO’s housing and household publication index provides further releases and updates. Check the geography before treating a national or regional finding as local.

Earlier census data and comparing change

Census 2016 Small Area Population Statistics provides historical local tables. Census 2011 SAPS also offers downloadable historical tables; its old mapping software may no longer work. Use the CSO census collection for other census years.

CSO introduced Built Up Areas in 2022. They are not directly comparable with the 2016 settlement boundaries. Check geography, question wording and population coverage before calculating a change; a boundary change can look like population growth.

03 / Understand what you are seeing

Information, disinformation or misinformation?

Check the evidence and context first. You can assess a claim even when you do not know why someone shared it.

Reliable information
Information supported by evidence that can be checked, with its date, source and relevant context made clear.
Misinformation
False or misleading information shared without an intention to deceive. Someone can share it while believing it is true.
Disinformation
False or misleading information deliberately created or spread to deceive, cause harm or gain an advantage.

A genuine photo can still mislead when given a false date or location. The Council of Europe also distinguishes malinformation: genuine information used to cause harm. An error alone does not prove deliberate deception, and an unverified claim is not automatically false. See the frameworks below.

  1. Council of Europe

    2017 · Research framework

    Information Disorder ↗

    Understand the distinctions

    Claire Wardle and Hossein Derakhshan’s framework explains how inaccurate or harmful information is created and shared, and why context and intention matter.

    A foundational framework; its examples predate today’s generative AI tools.

  2. UNESCO

    2018 handbook · Page updated 2024 · Multilingual

    Journalism, “Fake News” & Disinformation ↗

    Learn how verification works

    Training modules on information disorder, fact-checking and verifying sources. Useful for educators, community communicators and anyone who wants a more systematic approach.

    Read the verification methods as durable skills; specific platform examples reflect the handbook’s publication period.

  3. European Commission

    Living resource · European Union · Explainer

    Tackling Online Disinformation ↗

    Recognise deliberate deception

    An official explanation of misinformation and disinformation, the harms they can cause, and the EU’s response. A useful reference when the terms are used interchangeably.

    Policy context and definitions; it does not fact-check each claim circulating in a local group.

04 / Build your checking skills

how can I tell what is real?

You do not need to be an expert to ask useful questions. These three resources help turn a moment of doubt into a practical checking habit.

  1. Media Literacy Ireland

    Ireland · Practical checklist

    Be Media Smart: tips ↗

    A quick everyday habit

    The Stop, Think, Check approach helps you pause, look beyond a headline, examine the source and check images and claims before sharing.

    A clear starting point for all ages and levels of digital confidence.

  2. Digital Inquiry Group

    Research-based learning · International use

    Civic Online Reasoning ↗

    Practise checking outside the page

    Lessons and activities on who is behind information, what evidence supports it and what other sources say. Learn lateral reading: leave a page to investigate its credibility elsewhere.

    Developed in a US education context; the checking skills can be used with Irish sources too.

  3. Webwise · Irish Internet Safety Awareness Centre

    Ireland · Junior Cycle & educators

    Connected ↗

    Build digital literacy with young people

    Teaching materials about news and false information, online wellbeing, data and digital rights. Includes English and Irish resources and a dedicated false-information module.

    Designed for teachers and young people; families and community educators can also explore the materials.

Before you share, try these five checks

  1. Pause. Notice if a post makes you feel angry, frightened or rushed. Read beyond its headline.
  2. Find the source. Who made the claim? Open the original report or statement, rather than relying on a screenshot.
  3. Check the context. Look for the date, place, definitions and comparison. Is an old story being presented as new?
  4. Look elsewhere. Check independent reporting or research. Several accounts copying the same post are not several independent sources.
  5. Check images and uncertainty. Look for an earlier version or use reverse image search. Appearance alone cannot prove authenticity. If you cannot verify a claim, do not share it as established fact.

These habits draw on Be Media Smart and Civic Online Reasoning. Apply them to claims that support your own views too.
